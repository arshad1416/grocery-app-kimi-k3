# PantryRun / GroceryApp — Comparative Audit of Two Independent Repositories

Scope: two working trees, audited across seven dimensions, every finding adversarially verified.

- **Repo A — `pantryrun`**: `github.com/arshad1416/pantryrun` @ `a0d11a4` (17 commits, branch `main`)
- **Repo B — `grocery-app`**: `github.com/arshad1416/grocery-app` @ `f11a4b3` (162 commits, branch `main`)

Every `file:line` reference below is relative to its own **repository root** — e.g.
`relay-server/server.js:809` means that path inside whichever repo the finding names.
Audited from fresh clones of the two commits above; re-clone at those SHAs to reproduce.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Neither repository is shippable today, and the single most important conclusion is that the highest-severity defect is one they share:** the blind-RSA contribution token's single-use guarantee is enforced against the malleable base64 *transport string* rather than the signed bytes, in both lineages. `pool-server.js:252` and `:280` in pantryrun, `:261` and `:289` in grocery-app, hand `usedTokensStore.checkAndMark()` the raw `Bearer` string, while signature verification runs over the decoded 288 bytes. Re-encoding the identical signed bytes in the base64url alphabet, or inserting whitespace at any of roughly 380 positions, produces a different string that verifies identically and misses the spent-set lookup. This was reproduced empirically against 288 random bytes. Both relays route `/api/pool/*` unconditionally from the main listener (pantryrun `server.js:847-848`, grocery-app `server.js:721-722`), so it is live in the default deployment of both. pantryrun applied exactly the right reasoning to invite replay — keying on `${familyId}:${nonce}` at `server.js:730-731` — and never carried it across to tokens. Fixing it is roughly a two-line change in each repo and it is the best risk-retired-per-unit-of-work item in this entire report.

**pantryrun is the safer repository to ship**, and it is not close on the server side. Its relay carries the full hardening from commit `7fb69b5`; grocery-app's relay carries none of it. Every one of those seven defects is present, unmitigated and byte-for-byte, in grocery-app today, and I verified there is no equivalent fix under a different filename: there is no `familyFounderKeys` registry, no `lib/collect-body.js` or any shared body cap, no authentication on `/stats`, and `tokens/used-tokens-store.js` still runs a live 24-hour pruning timer keyed on raw token strings.

**What blocks pantryrun** is a short, tractable list. First, `GroceryApp/src/sync/y-websocket.ts:433` reads an undefined libsodium constant, which — through its own offline-queue loader guard at `offline-queue-store.ts:97` — silently discards every offline edit on app restart; the fix is one character-for-character line change to `const abytes = 16;`. Second, the shared token-malleability defect above. Third, it has no secret scanning at any layer (no `.gitleaks.toml`, no CI job, no pre-commit hook) and a root `.gitignore` that leaves `.env.production`, `*.pem`, `*.keystore`, `*.p8`, `*.p12` all committable — a prevention gap in a repository whose own notes exist because a credential once leaked. None of these are architectural; all three are a day of work.

**What blocks grocery-app** is structural. Its relay Docker image cannot boot at all: `relay-server/Dockerfile:20` copies only `server.js`, while `server.js:26` requires `./tokens/used-tokens-store` at module scope, so the container dies with `MODULE_NOT_FOUND` before any listener binds and then crash-loops forever under `restart: unless-stopped`. Anyone who runs the relay directly with `node server.js` — the documented self-host and dev path — instead gets a server where any party holding a familyId can self-sign an invite and enroll into that family (`server.js:611-615` verifies the invite signature against the public key embedded in the invite itself), where spent blind tokens become spendable again 24 hours later, where `/stats` enumerates connected families and device fingerprints to any origin, and where three POST bodies are buffered without any cap. That is not a list of patches; it is the whole security commit, and grocery-app's CI is green throughout because its only relay test file is byte-identical to pantryrun's *pre*-hardening one.

**Recommendation: make pantryrun the base.** Port grocery-app's prevention tooling and its two client fixes into it, rather than porting seven security fixes plus a Dockerfile into grocery-app. Section 7 orders the work.

## 2. What these two repositories actually are

The two repositories share **zero git commits**. `git merge-base` returns nothing and no SHA appears in both object stores. They are independent lineages, not branches of one another, and no statement of the form "N commits behind" applies in either direction. Comparison here is between working trees and file contents.

pantryrun's root commit `7d4148e` is dated 2026-07-27 and is titled `chore: establish A/B/C comparison baseline` — a post-exposure squashed baseline, seventeen commits deep, whose history never contained the Turso credential that occasioned the original incident. grocery-app's 162-commit history is the older, longer line, and it *was* rewritten: the `git-filter-repo` marker `***REMOVED-REVOKED-TURSO-TOKEN***` survives in three blobs (`2e9bac79` and `86edbf4a`, both `GroceryApp/App.tsx`; `a343a897`, `.gitleaks.toml`), reproducible in commit `82f8271`. A full-object scan of both stores — 771 objects in pantryrun, 2273 in grocery-app, matched against strict and broad JWT patterns, `libsql://`, `ghp_`, `github_pat_`, `AKIA`, `BEGIN * PRIVATE KEY`, `xox*` and `AIza` — found **no live credential in either repository**, and `git fsck --unreachable --dangling` is clean in both. Two premises of the audit brief are therefore wrong and should not be carried forward: grocery-app's history is not unrewritten, and pantryrun's history never carried the leak. That verdict is scoped to these clones; remote GitHub state (forks, objects reachable by SHA, PR refs) is unobservable from a clone.

Functionally the two are the same product at different postures. Both are Expo/React Native clients named PantryRun with a Node relay-server, both declare bundle identifier `com.shiftlogichq.pantryrun`, both use the same WatermelonDB database name `groceryapp`, and large parts of the client are byte-identical — `crypto/index.ts`, `identity/device.ts`, `identity/enroll.ts`, `identity/family.ts`, `identity/invite-link.ts`, `tokens.ts`, `contribute.ts`, `offline-queue-store.ts`, `relay-server/doc-store.js` and `README.md` all diff clean.

They diverged in substance along three axes.

**Server security.** pantryrun's commit `7fb69b5` hardened the relay against seven named defects and added `lib/collect-body.js`, `hardening.test.js` and `verify-hardening.js`. None of that exists in grocery-app's tree, and the vulnerable behavior is in some cases actively pinned green by grocery-app's own tests: `relay-server/tokens/__tests__/token-issuer.test.js:382` asserts `expect(store.isUsed('expired-token')).toBe(false)` — that is, it asserts the double-spend window as intended behavior — where pantryrun's same file at `:382` asserts the opposite.

**Product posture.** pantryrun ships a paid tier: `GroceryApp/src/config/entitlements.ts` computes `isPlus` from a receipt with a matching productId, non-empty transactionId and unexpired window, and `GroceryListScreen.tsx:1069` renders the Trip Optimizer for Plus users with a visible upsell at `:1089`. grocery-app has no `entitlements.ts` at all, declares v1 fully free (`GroceryApp/docs/MONETIZATION.md:3`), and compiles the Trip Optimizer out entirely (`GroceryListScreen.tsx:74`, `const TRIP_OPTIMIZER_ENABLED = false`). Conversely grocery-app kept the Turso-backed catalog alive — a whole `relay-server/catalog/` module, a `catalogClient.ts`, a Deals tab, and `settings.ts:48 tursoEnabled: true` — where pantryrun deleted the toggle and the surface outright.

**Supply-chain hygiene.** grocery-app built prevention that pantryrun never did: a `.gitleaks.toml`, a CI `secrets` job over full history, a `native-api-fidelity.test.ts` that mechanically diffs every `sodium.*` reference in `src/` against the real native module's declarations, a `no-client-db-credential.test.ts`, and a `babel-decorators.test.ts`. pantryrun has none of these; its equivalent guarantees rest on rewritten source and unenforced convention.

## 3. Critical and high findings

Findings are grouped by severity. Where two dimensions independently reached the same underlying defect, they are merged into one entry and the corroboration is stated.

### CRITICAL

#### C1. Blind-token single-use is keyed on the malleable transport string — unlimited double-spend
**Repos: both.** Dimension: crypto-identity.

`pool-server.js:252` (pantryrun) and `:261` (grocery-app) take `tokenStr` as the trimmed `Bearer` value; `:280` and `:289` respectively pass that string to `usedTokensStore.checkAndMark()`. Parsing is lenient — `Buffer.from(encoded, 'base64')` at pantryrun `pool-server.js:107` and grocery-app `:106` — and verification runs over the *decoded* bytes via `_blindRsaSuite.verify(_publicKey, parsed.signature, parsed.nonce)`. pantryrun's hashing at `tokens/used-tokens-store.js:113` does not help: `hashToken(tokenStr)` hashes the same malleable string. Malleability was reproduced independently in node against 288 random bytes — standard base64, base64url (`+/` → `-_`), and whitespace-injected variants all decode to identical 288 bytes while the strings differ.

*Failure scenario.* A contributor obtains one legitimately issued token, spends it, then re-encodes the same signed bytes in base64url and spends it again, and again, indefinitely. The signature verifies every time; the spent-set lookup never matches. A single device poisons the shared price pool without limit, defeating the entire abuse-control premise the blind-RSA design exists to provide. pantryrun's own hardening suite never touches this — `hardening.test.js:261` and `:271` test only record permanence — so commit `7fb69b5` did not close it.

*Fix.* Key the spent set on the signed value at `pool-server.js:280` / `:289`: `usedTokensStore.checkAndMark(Buffer.from(parsed.nonce).toString('hex'))`, or SHA-256 over `signature || nonce` bytes. Belt-and-braces, reject non-canonical transport in `parseToken` by re-encoding the decoded bytes and comparing against `encoded`.

#### C2. grocery-app's relay Docker image cannot boot — MODULE_NOT_FOUND at require time
**Repo: grocery-app.** Independently corroborated by four dimensions (relay-security, persistence-sync, build-release, quality-tests-docs).

`relay-server/Dockerfile` has exactly two source COPY lines: `COPY package.json ./` at `:15` and `COPY server.js ./` at `:20`. `relay-server/server.js:26` is a top-level `require('./tokens/used-tokens-store')`, with further top-level requires at `:237` (`./pool/store`), `:238` (`./pool/pool-server`) and `:239` (`./seed-pool`), plus lazy ones at `:727`, `:736` and `:954`. None of those paths are copied into the image. `docker-compose.yml:11-13` builds from this Dockerfile rather than pulling an image, and `:19` sets `restart: unless-stopped` with a healthcheck at `:83-88`. pantryrun's counterpart is correct: `relay-server/Dockerfile:25-29` is an explicit allowlist (`COPY server.js seed-pool.js encrypted-store.js ./` plus `lib/`, `tokens/`, `pool/`, `extract/`) with the rationale written out at `:19-24`.

*Failure scenario.* `docker compose up` produces a green build and a container that dies at module load before binding port 8080, then crash-loops forever while the healthcheck never passes. Every client's `connect()` fails; updates pile into the in-memory queue until `MAX_QUEUE_SIZE=1000` and the oldest are shifted off (`y-websocket.ts:484`) — and because neither client has any full-state reconciliation (see H10), those are permanently lost rather than deferred. CI cannot catch it: `grocery-app/.github/workflows/ci.yml:133-143` only builds the image and never runs it.

*Fix, with an ordering constraint.* Port pantryrun's COPY allowlist from `Dockerfile:25-29` — never `COPY . ./`, since `keys/` and local state live in the build context. **Port pantryrun's `.dockerignore` in the same change.** grocery-app's `.dockerignore` is exactly five lines (`node_modules`, `npm-debug.log`, `.git`, `.gitignore`, `*.md`) against pantryrun's `:6-21` which excludes `keys/`, `data/`, `relay-state.json`, `used-tokens.json`, `relay-docs.json`, `.env`, `tokens/__tests__/` and `extract/*.bak*`. Widening the COPY without first porting the ignore file would bake `keys/issuer-private-key.pem` into a published image. Add a smoke step that boots the built image and polls `/health`; neither repo has one.

#### C3. grocery-app verifies invite signatures against the key embedded in the invite — anyone can self-sign into any family
**Repo: grocery-app.** Independently corroborated by relay-security, crypto-identity, build-release and quality-tests-docs.

`server.js:586` destructures `deviceId: inviterDeviceId` out of the attacker-supplied invite; `:604-609` rebuilds the signed payload from those same fields; `:611-615` passes `inviterDeviceId` as the third argument to `verifyEd25519Signature`, whose third parameter is the *verifying public key* (`nacl.sign.detached.verify(msgBytes, sigBytes, keyBytes)`, `server.js:396-407`). The verifier key is therefore fully attacker-chosen. Walking `:617` through `:677` line by line, there is an expiry check, a replay check at `:626`, a `MAX_FAMILIES` check at `:633` and a per-family device check at `:641` — and no lookup of `inviterDeviceId` against any server-side record before `:652` mints the relayToken and `:667` binds it to the familyId. `grep -n familyFounderKeys` over grocery-app's `server.js` returns zero hits and there is no renamed equivalent. That `deviceId` really is the Ed25519 public key is confirmed client-side at `GroceryApp/src/identity/family.ts:111-114`, a file byte-identical between the repos.

*Failure scenario.* A familyId travels in every invite QR and deep link, including expired ones, and grocery-app's unauthenticated `/stats` echoes connected familyIds (see H4). The attacker mints a throwaway Ed25519 keypair, self-signs `{familyId, deviceId: <own pubkey>, expiresAt, nonce}`, POSTs `/enroll`, and receives a valid relayToken bound to the victim family. From there: WebSocket auth, membership in `familyRooms`, receipt of every relayed Yjs update and device metadata, update injection, contribution-token minting as that family, and device-slot exhaustion against `MAX_DEVICES_PER_FAMILY=20` (`:642`). List plaintext stays protected under XChaCha20, so the ceiling is unauthorized room membership plus full metadata and write access, not plaintext disclosure — which is still an unauthenticated authorization bypass reachable from a value designed to be shared.

*Fix.* Port pantryrun's `familyFounderKeys` registry: declaration at `server.js:462`, enforcement at `:719-723`, population at `:761-763`, and persistence hooks at `:292`/`:300` and `:351-353`. Note the ceiling pantryrun documents at `server.js:455-476`: `/enroll` only ever receives the joiner's X25519 box key, never its Ed25519 signing key, so the registry can only hold *founding* signers — after porting, only the founding device can issue accepted invites until the client also sends its signing key.

#### C4. grocery-app prunes spent blind tokens after 24 hours, and the token format carries no expiry
**Repo: grocery-app.** Independently corroborated by crypto-identity and relay-security (defect 7). *Severity split: crypto-identity graded it critical, relay-security graded it high; critical is taken here because the token format carries no expiry the verifier could fall back on.*

`relay-server/tokens/used-tokens-store.js:18` sets `DEFAULT_TTL_MS = 86_400_000`, `:31` assigns it, `:53-59` drops entries older than the TTL on load, `:118-132` is `cleanExpired()` deleting entries and re-saving, `:166-178` runs it on a `setInterval`, and `:73` starts that timer from `loadOnStartup()` — the pruning is live, not dormant. The wire format carries no time field (`pool-server.js:83-84` documents it; `parseToken` at `:89-120` reads none) and verification at `pool-server.js:270-285` checks only the blind-RSA signature over a client-chosen nonce, so the verifier cannot distinguish an old token from a new one. Tokens are also keyed and written in raw spendable form (`:88`, `:92`, `:144` `fs.writeFileSync`). pantryrun closed it: `cleanExpired()` is `return 0` with the reasoning in the module doc at `:7-19`, records are permanent and SHA-256-hashed at `:113-118`, there is no cleanup timer, and a legacy raw-key migration upgrades existing files in place.

*Failure scenario.* A device mints its 10 tokens/hour (`server.js:40 TOKEN_RATE_LIMIT`, enforced at `:1430`), spends them, waits 24 hours, and replays the identical strings — the records are gone, the signature still verifies, contributions are accepted. Repeat forever, no encoding trick required. Compounds with C2: because grocery-app writes state inside the container layer with no volume (M4), a rebuild wipes the entire spent set immediately, which is a *faster* path to double-spend than waiting out the TTL. The at-rest half is real too — cleartext `v2.<base64>` tokens in `used-tokens.json` are recoverable and become spendable again once their TTL lapses.

*Fix.* Port pantryrun's `used-tokens-store.js` wholesale, including the load-time prune removal at grocery-app `:53-59`, and flip `tokens/__tests__/token-issuer.test.js:382-402` to assert `isUsed` stays true.

#### C5. pantryrun's Yjs wire encryption reads an undefined constant, and its own offline-queue loader then discards every persisted offline edit
**Repo: pantryrun.** Dimensions: persistence-sync (CONFIRMED critical) and crypto-identity (PLAUSIBLE high). **Verdict split — see the caveat.**

`GroceryApp/src/sync/y-websocket.ts:433` is `const abytes = sodium.crypto_aead_xchacha20poly1305_ietf_ABYTES;` with the two slices at `:434-435`. grocery-app fixed the same line at `:441` with the literal `16` and an explanatory comment at `:433-440`. pantryrun's own `crypto/index.ts:61` hardcodes 16 for the same AEAD and uses it identically at `:214-215`. If `ABYTES` is undefined at runtime, `length - undefined` is `NaN`, `slice(0, NaN)` returns empty and `slice(NaN)` returns the whole buffer, so every envelope carries `ciphertext: ""` with cipher||tag in the `tag` field. The loss chain is complete: `y-websocket.ts:490` calls `persistEntry`, `:497-506` encrypts and calls `saveQueueEntry`, and on relaunch `restorePersistedQueue` hits `offline-queue-store.ts:97` — `if (!payload?.ciphertext || !payload?.iv || !payload?.tag) continue;` — where base64 of a zero-length array is `""`, which is falsy. Every row is skipped.

*The caveat.* The runtime premise — that `crypto_aead_xchacha20poly1305_ietf_ABYTES` is genuinely absent from `react-native-libsodium ^1.7.0` on device — could not be executed, because `node_modules` is absent from both trees. It is corroborated structurally rather than reproduced. Three pieces of support: the same function trusts `NPUBBYTES` ten lines earlier at `:423`, and if constants were blanket-undefined `randombytes_buf(undefined)` would throw and fail loudly in *both* repos, so the absence must be member-specific; grocery-app's `__tests__/native-api-fidelity.test.ts` mechanically diffs `sodium.*` references against the real module's type declarations with a single waiver (`crypto_scalarmult_base`) and passes while still referencing `NPUBBYTES` at `y-websocket.ts:423` — which is exactly the member-specific pattern required; and pantryrun independently documents the same bug class at `src/identity/recovery.ts:49`, where `sodium.crypto_hash_sha256` is confirmed undefined on device and worked around with pure-TS SHA-256 at `:351`/`:413`. The test mock cannot see any of this: `__mocks__/react-native-libsodium.js:9-10` is a two-line re-export of `libsodium-wrappers`, byte-identical in both repos.

*Failure scenario.* A user edits offline, the row is written, they force-quit before delivery, and on next launch every queued row is silently skipped — in the queue whose entire stated purpose is to prevent exactly that. Live device-to-device sync still round-trips because `decryptUpdate` at `:452-457` re-concatenates, and the relay accepts it because `encrypted-store.js:57` only checks `typeof update.ciphertext !== 'string'` — which is why nothing else fails loudly. Neither client has any full-state reconciliation (H10), so that update is gone from the family permanently rather than eventually reconciled. Secondary: skipped rows are neither loaded nor deleted, so the `offline_queue` table grows without bound.

*Fix.* `y-websocket.ts:433` → `const abytes = 16;`, matching grocery-app and pantryrun's own `crypto/index.ts:61`. The fix is free and correct regardless of how the runtime question resolves. Settle the premise separately with `Object.keys(require('react-native-libsodium')).filter(k => /ABYTES/.test(k))` on an installed tree. Port grocery-app's `native-api-fidelity.test.ts` so the bug class is caught mechanically, and make `offline-queue-store.ts:97` report-and-delete instead of silently continuing.

#### C6. grocery-app's CI is green while the entire relay hardening commit is absent
**Repo: grocery-app.** Dimensions: build-release and quality-tests-docs (the same roll-up reached twice).

This is the umbrella over C2, C3, C4 and H1–H4: all seven defects that pantryrun's `7fb69b5` fixed are present in grocery-app's tree, and nothing in its test suite detects them. `relay-server/server.test.js` is **byte-identical** to pantryrun's pre-hardening copy (`diff -q` returns 0, 183 lines) and asserts nothing about `/stats` auth, invite replay, or body size. `hardening.test.js`, `verify-hardening.js` and `lib/collect-body.js` are all absent from grocery-app. Both `package.json` test scripts are plain jest with default `testMatch`, so a ported `hardening.test.js` would be picked up automatically — the vulnerable behavior is not locked in by a test, it is simply untested. Reachability note that matters for triage: the relay's `package.json` ships `"start": "node server.js"`, and this is a self-host product, so running the relay outside Docker is a first-class supported path. **The Docker boot failure does not make the other six defects moot.**

*Fix.* Port `hardening.test.js` and `verify-hardening.js` alongside the code fixes so they cannot regress, and add a container smoke test to both repos' CI.

### HIGH

#### H1. grocery-app checks enrollment capacity against a map only the WebSocket path populates
**Repo: grocery-app.** Dimension: relay-security (defect 3).

`server.js:633-638` computes `const totalFamilies = familyRooms.size` and rejects at `>= MAX_FAMILIES`. Enumerating every `familyRooms` site in the file — `:347` declaration, `:422`/`:426` removeClient, `:521` `/health`, `:633` the check, `:692`/`:704` `/stats`, `:1000`, `:1228`, `:1237-1238`, `:1241`, `:1332` — the **only** `.set` is at `:1238`, inside the WebSocket `identity` handler. The enrollment path writes `familyDeviceTokens` at `:664-667` instead. An enroll-only attacker therefore leaves `familyRooms.size` at 0 and the cap is unreachable. pantryrun's `server.js:742` reads `if (!familyDeviceTokens.has(familyId) && familyDeviceTokens.size >= MAX_FAMILIES)`.

*Failure scenario.* Chained with C3, a script POSTing `/enroll` with a fresh random familyId per request grows `enrolledDevices` (`:656`) and `familyDeviceTokens` (`:664`) without bound; `MAX_FAMILIES=100` (`docker-compose.yml:28`) is never consulted; growth continues to the 256M container limit (`docker-compose.yml:99`) and an OOM kill takes every real family's sync down. On-disk growth is aggregate rather than per-request — `persistState` is debounced at 500ms (`server.js:314-324`) — but `relay-state.json` still grows monotonically. The counter is wrong in both directions: a legitimate second device can also be refused once `familyRooms` happens to hold `MAX_FAMILIES` live rooms.

*Fix.* Replace `server.js:633-638` with pantryrun's form at `:742`.

#### H2. grocery-app keys invite replay on the raw JSON string — re-serialization defeats one-time use
**Repo: grocery-app.** Corroborated by relay-security (defect 4, high) and crypto-identity (which downgraded to medium on overlap grounds, see below).

`server.js:381` declares `const usedInviteSignatures = new Set()`; `:626` checks `.has(familyInviteToken)` and `:649` `.add(familyInviteToken)` — the raw client-supplied string, round-tripped through persistence at `:268` and `:293-295`. The bypass works because `:604-609` rebuilds the signed payload with a *fixed* key order, so reordering keys or adding whitespace in the outer JSON yields a different outer string but an identical rebuilt payload: the Ed25519 check at `:611-615` still passes while the `:626` lookup misses. Separately, `:588` does not require a nonce and `:608`'s `...(nonce !== undefined ? { nonce } : {})` accepts nonce-less invites. pantryrun uses a Map at `:442` keyed `${familyId}:${nonce}` (`:730-735`), hard-requires the nonce at `:673-680`, includes it unconditionally at `:697`, marks with the invite's own `expiresAt` at `:758`, and prunes numerically at `:1671-1673`.

*Severity note.* The relay-security verifier graded this high on its own merits; the crypto-identity verifier downgraded it to medium on the grounds that in today's grocery-app an attacker who wants a second enrollment simply self-signs a fresh invite (C3), so this grants nothing extra. Both are right: it is dominated by C3 today and becomes the top invite defect the moment C3 is fixed. Its independent value right now is against an attacker who merely *captured* a legitimate invite. Fix it in the same change as C3 — C3's registry and the nonce requirement at `:673-680` are prerequisites anyway. The re-serialization path is the load-bearing half; the missing-nonce check is defense in depth.

#### H3. grocery-app buffers whole request bodies before checking size; three endpoints have no check at all
**Repo: grocery-app.** Dimension: relay-security (defect 5), corroborated by build-release and quality-tests-docs.

`req.on('data'` sites in grocery-app: `server.js:555`, `:836`, `:872`, `:986`, `:1440`; `pool/pool-server.js:235`; plus `extract/extract-server.js:178` and `catalog/catalog-server.js:378`, which are local stream-capping `collectBody` copies at `:167-190` and `:367-391` and are **not** gaps. Length guards exist only at `server.js:558`, `server.js:1443` and `pool-server.js:299` — all inside the `end` handler, i.e. buffer-then-reject, never calling `req.destroy()`. `/api/assistant/submit-update` at `:985-1000` has no length check anywhere. `grocery-app/relay-server/lib/` does not exist. pantryrun's shared `lib/collect-body.js:19-43` does a Content-Length pre-check, a running byte count and `req.destroy()` on exceed, wired at `server.js:635`, `:952`, `:991`, `:1110`, `:1567` and `pool/pool-server.js:290`.

*Failure scenario, with the anchor corrected.* Three of the six sites — `:836` `/api/oauth/pair`, `:872` `/oauth/token`, `:986` submit-update — sit behind the `ASSISTANT_INTEGRATION` gate (`:187`, default false; 404 branch at `:742-750`), so the truly uncapped endpoint is the worst per-request but requires an opt-in flag plus a valid Bearer token. What justifies high is the reachable-by-default trio: `/enroll` (`:555`) is completely unauthenticated, `handleTokenRequest` (`:1440`) and pool contribute (`pool-server.js:235`, published on port 8081) are effectively reachable, and all three buffer the entire body into a JS string before the 4096 check runs. A few hundred megabytes POSTed to `/enroll` with `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` is fully resident before rejection, OOM-killing a 256M container and taking sync down for every family; `restart: unless-stopped` makes it repeatable rather than permanent.

*Fix.* Copy `lib/collect-body.js` into grocery-app and replace all six sites with `collectBody(req, N)` + `if (body === null) return 413`. Prioritise `:555`, `:1440` and `pool-server.js:235`. Folding the two local copies into the shared module is hygiene, not a fix.

#### H4. grocery-app's `/stats` is unauthenticated, enumerates familyIds and device fingerprints, and is readable cross-origin
**Repo: grocery-app.** Dimension: relay-security (defect 6), corroborated by build-release and quality-tests-docs.

`server.js:688-715` opens with `res.writeHead(200, ...)` at `:689` with no Authorization handling in the branch; `:691-700` builds per-family `{connectedClients, deviceIds: [...deviceId.slice(0,12)+'...']}`; `:703-715` emits it as `families: stats`. The wildcard `res.setHeader('Access-Control-Allow-Origin', '*')` at `:505-507` is applied unconditionally before route dispatch and never removed on this route. Port 8080 is published (`docker-compose.yml:21`), and the `createServer` wrapper at `:487-502` only selects https vs http on TLS env vars, so the route is reachable. pantryrun's fix is effective, not cosmetic: `server.js:586` sets the wildcard with `setHeader` (not `writeHead`), dispatch begins after, and `:811` `res.removeHeader(...)` runs before `:829 writeHead` — so headers are still mutable and the removal lands. It then requires `Bearer <relayToken>` validated against `enrolledDevices` with an expiry check (`:813-825`) and returns aggregates only (`:829-843`).

*Failure scenario.* Any unauthenticated caller gets the currently-connected familyIds — precisely the value C3 needs to self-sign an invite — plus a stable 12-hex-char prefix of every connected device's public key, a persistent cross-session fingerprint. Because the wildcard CORS header survives on this route, any web page a family member visits can fetch and read it from the browser, so this is not merely a network-perimeter issue. One honesty correction: it iterates `familyRooms`, so it enumerates families with a *live* WebSocket connection, not every enrolled family.

*Fix.* Port `server.js:811-843` verbatim. Note `/health` leaks the same aggregate counters unauthenticated in **both** repos, so this fix is incomplete on its own.

#### H5. OAuth assistant pairing: 6-digit `Math.random()` codes, unauthenticated unrate-limited polling, sessions that never expire, unvalidated redirect_uri
**Repos: both.** Dimension: relay-security. This is the one place neither lineage is ahead.

`generatePairingCodeString` is byte-identical (pantryrun `server.js:247-253`, grocery-app `:226-232`): six `Math.floor(Math.random()*10)` digits, non-CSPRNG, 10^6 space. `/oauth/authorize` takes `redirect_uri` and `state` straight from the query with no allowlist and stores them (pantryrun `:888-917` / grocery-app `:770-799`). `/oauth/status` is unauthenticated and unrate-limited and returns `${session.redirectUri}...code=${session.authCode}&state=...` on a linked session (pantryrun `:920-948` / grocery-app `:802-830`). `/api/oauth/pair` accepts any familyId and encryptedMasterKey for a known code with no auth (pantryrun `:951-987` / grocery-app `:833-865`). `/oauth/token` scans all sessions for the authCode with no client_secret and no redirect_uri match, and mints a 1-year token (pantryrun `:1018-1049` / grocery-app `:897-920`). `createdAt` is written once (pantryrun `:911` / grocery-app `:794`) and read nowhere, so sessions that never complete an exchange are never evicted.

*Failure scenario.* Gated: with `ASSISTANT_INTEGRATION` unset (the default, pantryrun `:208` / grocery-app `:187`) every `/oauth/`, `/api/oauth/` and `/api/assistant/` route 404s (pantryrun `:859-868` / grocery-app `:742-750`). On an opt-in deploy the chain is complete: enumerate all 10^6 pairing codes against unauthenticated, unrate-limited `/oauth/status` against a session set that is never pruned; take any hit's authCode to `/oauth/token` with no client authentication; receive a 1-year Bearer token bound to the victim's familyId; read the wrapped `encryptedMasterKey` plus the entire stored ciphertext corpus from `/api/assistant/list-data` and inject updates via submit-update. The master key is wrapped for the assistant keypair, so the ceiling is bulk ciphertext and metadata exfiltration plus write access, not direct plaintext. Kept at high because flipping an operator flag is a supported, documented configuration.

*Fix.* Mint pairing codes with `crypto.randomInt` over a larger alphabet (≥8 chars); give `oauthSessions` a hard 5–10 minute TTL swept by the existing hourly cleanup (`createdAt` is already recorded and just needs reading); rate-limit and lock out per-IP guessing on `/oauth/status` and `/api/oauth/pair`; bind `/oauth/token` to a registered client_id/client_secret and require the redirect_uri to match the authorize request; validate redirect_uri against an operator allowlist at pantryrun `:890` / grocery-app `:773`. Do **not** bother making authCode single-use — it already is (pantryrun `:1041` / grocery-app `:918` both delete on exchange). `getAuthorizeHtml` (pantryrun `:1718`) was checked for reflected XSS and only interpolates the server-generated pairingCode; not a gap, recorded as checked.

#### H10. Neither client has any full-state reconciliation — every dropped update is permanent
**Repos: both.** Surfaced by the persistence-sync verifier as the structural fact underlying several separately-reported findings.

`encodeStateAsUpdate` and `encodeStateVector` return zero hits in either repo's `GroceryApp/src/sync/`. The client protocol union at `y-websocket.ts:543` is only `auth | auth_ack | identity | update | ack | error | notification`, and the only messages the client ever sends are auth (`:187`), identity (`:195`/`:377`), update (`:293`/`:339`) and notification (`:316`). The relay holds no merged CRDT either — no `require('yjs')`, no `Y.Doc`, no `applyUpdate` in either `server.js` — and only replays individually stored encrypted updates until `UPDATE_TTL_MS`.

*Why it matters.* This is the shared reason every drop path in this audit is *divergent forever* rather than *eventually reconciled*: C5's queue loss, C2's queue overflow at `y-websocket.ts:484`, the undecryptable-entry deletion at `:137`, the relay volume wipe (M4), a schema downgrade (M3), and plain TTL expiry. It is reported here because it converts a set of individually-medium durability issues into permanent data loss, and because it is the single design gap whose closure would defuse all of them at once.

*Fix.* Add a state-vector exchange on connect: client sends `encodeStateVector`, relay (or peer) replies with the missing delta. This is real work, not a patch, and belongs on the roadmap rather than the launch checklist — but it should be a known, named gap rather than an accident.

#### H11. Per-device token rate limiting is keyed on a self-asserted, never-authenticated identifier
**Repos: both, worse in grocery-app.** Surfaced by the crypto-identity verifier.

`/enroll` stores `deviceId: deviceToken` straight from the request body with no signature over it and no binding to any key (pantryrun `server.js:771`, grocery-app `:657`), and `handleTokenRequest` then rate-limits on exactly that value (`checkTokenRateLimit(deviceId)`, defined pantryrun `:132` / grocery-app `:113`, called from pantryrun `:1558` / grocery-app `:1430`). So `TOKEN_RATE_LIMIT` — nominally 10 tokens/hour/device (pantryrun `:57` / grocery-app `:40`) — is really 10/hour per string the client chose, multiplied by `MAX_DEVICES_PER_FAMILY = 20` (pantryrun `:170` / grocery-app `:151`) for anyone able to mint invites, and `createFamilyInvite` mints a fresh nonce per call (`GroceryApp/src/identity/family.ts:104-114`) so a founder has unlimited invites.

*Failure scenario.* In grocery-app this compounds with C3: forged invites give attacker-controlled enrollments, hence attacker-controlled deviceIds, hence an effectively unbounded issuance budget before any double-spend trick is even needed. This is the abuse-control anchor that C1 and C4 both sit on.

*Fix.* Bind the rate-limit key to the enrollment — the relayToken or a server-generated device handle — not to the client-supplied `deviceToken`.

## 4. Dimension-by-dimension comparison

**Relay security — pantryrun, decisively.** All seven defects fixed by `7fb69b5` remain live in grocery-app with no equivalent under another name. Two are launch-blocking alone (C2, C3). The reverse direction is empty: four candidate pantryrun-only weaknesses were probed and all four collapsed — founder-registry squatting is infeasible because familyId is a 122-bit `randombytes_buf(16)` UUID (`crypto/index.ts:168-179`, `identity/family.ts:99`); the `removeHeader` at `server.js:811` is genuinely effective; `checkAndMark` is synchronous in both repos (`used-tokens-store.js:108` / `:83`) so pool-server's non-awaited call is correct despite a stale docstring; and there is no path traversal in either, every `fs` path being a module constant. grocery-app's one clear lead is its Turso catalog layer, which pantryrun lacks entirely: `catalog/turso-client.js:99-137` uses a fixed QUERIES table with bound positional parameters and no passthrough, and `catalog-server.js:67-108`/`:280-311` validates every input and requires a Bearer relayToken with per-device rate limiting — no injection. Neither repo has SSRF in `extract/` (`ollama-client.js:16`, `qwen-client.js:17` read URLs from env only) and the WebSocket path is identical and sound in both.

**Secrets and history — grocery-app on prevention, but it drilled a hole in its own control.** grocery-app is the only repo with a scanner, a CI job over full history, a file-class `.gitignore`, and a structural removal guard (`no-client-db-credential.test.ts:65` asserts `tursoClient.ts` does not exist; `:82` asserts `/v2/pipeline` appears nowhere). pantryrun has none of it (H7, H8). But grocery-app's allowlist exempts entire directories (H6), which is the single worst defect in this dimension. The correct end state is grocery-app's tooling with the `paths` keys removed, ported into pantryrun. Both carry lesser residue: pantryrun retains a dormant `tursoClient.ts` behind a guard narrow enough to miss the obvious reintroduction path (M1), grocery-app still publishes the production Turso hostname at `GOAL_PROMPT_NOTES.md:410` (L2).

**Crypto and identity — pantryrun, with two inversions.** pantryrun's relay closes three token/identity holes grocery-app still ships (C3, C4, H2). The client crypto is nearly identical — `crypto/index.ts`, `device.ts`, `enroll.ts`, `family.ts`, `invite-link.ts`, `tokens.ts` and `contribute.ts` are byte-for-byte the same — so every client-side weakness is shared (L4, L5, I3, I4). Two places invert the ranking: grocery-app fixed the ABYTES bug pantryrun knowingly left (C5), and grocery-app's `native-api-fidelity.test.ts` is the mechanism that found it. On recovery the two are security-equivalent rather than better-and-worse — both root a 128-bit CSPRNG seed with a 4-bit typo digit, pantryrun via pure-TS SHA-256 and grocery-app via BLAKE2b — but the practical consequence is that phrases minted by one build will not validate on the other. pantryrun's `recovery-first-run.test.ts` is the strictly stronger test, adding a native-API-fidelity guard, NIST vectors across 14 input sizes, and the no-family-membership branch. And one critical defect is genuinely shared and unfixed (C1).

**Persistence and sync — split, close to complementary.** grocery-app wins client-side: it fixed the ABYTES constant that silently loses every persisted offline edit in pantryrun (C5). pantryrun wins server-side: grocery-app's relay cannot boot (C2), and once that is fixed it still writes durable state inside the image with no volume (M4) where pantryrun routes everything through `RELAY_DATA_DIR` onto a named volume. Migration coverage is genuinely clean in both — every version bump has a matching non-destructive step — but the schema versions have diverged to 6 and 5 under an identical bundle identifier and dbName with no downgrade detection anywhere (M3). Worth recording as a checked non-finding: `diff` of the two `schema.ts` files yields exactly the version line and the entitlements block, and `diff` of the two `migrations.ts` yields exactly the toVersion-6 step, so every shared table and column is byte-identical — there is no same-version/different-shape divergence, which would have been strictly worse since no version number would ever signal it. The two Hermes-decorator arrangements differ but are both currently correct, and the `persist-error-surfacing` / `persist-failure-surfacing` pair is a rename with equivalent assertions, not a gap — the real surfacing gaps are shared (M6).

**Build and release — grocery-app is materially riskier to ship.** Its relay image cannot boot (C2), its CI is green across the whole missing hardening commit (C6), it has a functional deep-link bug (H9), and it ships Siri/mic/speech entitlements for a feature with zero native backing (M9). pantryrun's problems are configuration debt: an app.json cleartext key that may be a no-op (M8), iOS pinned to buildNumber 1 with autoIncrement off (M7), a dead `sentry-expo` dependency adding five advisories (L8, reproduced: 35 total vulnerabilities vs grocery-app's 30, delta = `@sentry/browser`, `@sentry/react`, `@sentry/react-native`, `postcss`, `sentry-expo`), and a `babel.config.js` still carrying the decorator misconfiguration grocery-app diagnosed and fixed, dormant only because `models.ts` was rewritten around it (L7). One finding cuts against the framing: cleartext HTTP is enabled unscoped in **both** committed Android manifests (M5). Not deep-dived in this pass: Podfile.lock/pbxproj full diffs and PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy beyond a top-level diff — grocery-app's Podfile adds a post_install hook disabling code-signing on resource-only CocoaPods bundles, and pantryrun's PrivacyInfo declares three extra API-reason codes.

**Product gating — pantryrun, clearly.** pantryrun removed the entire Turso/catalog surface for v1, keeps every data-leaving-device toggle off by default (`priceServiceEnabled`, `pricingOptedIn`, `cloudFlyerEnabled`, `contributeEnabled` all false at `settings.ts:43-49`), and gates its one paid feature behind a real receipt-validated entitlement that holds in source with no second render path. grocery-app ships v1 fully free with the optimizer compiled out — also verified in source — but kept the catalog client alive and defaulted it on (`settings.ts:48 tursoEnabled: true`, a key pantryrun does not have at all), and fires an unconsented catalog GET on every launch (M10). Both agree on `MANAGED_TIER_ENABLED = false`. Deliberately not reported as a finding: pantryrun's `computeIsPlus` (`entitlements.ts:96-102`) does not distinguish `source: 'sync'` from `'purchase'`, so a family member could inject an entitlement record — but the module header at `:12-26` disposes of this explicitly, one purchase unlocks the family by design, and client-side gating being bypassable in a public repo is the recorded owner stance.

**Quality, tests and docs — split.** grocery-app is ahead on the persistence bug class (all three faults from its own pre-launch audit are genuinely fixed, with `fresh-install-persistence.test.ts` and a writer-enforcing mock to prove it) and on doc/reality convergence generally. pantryrun is ahead on relay security and deployability. Test parity runs both ways once the known renames are applied: grocery-app uniquely has `native-api-fidelity.test.ts`, `no-client-db-credential.test.ts`, `settings-schema.test.ts` and `babel-decorators.test.ts`; pantryrun uniquely has `delete-all-data.test.ts`, `entitlements.test.ts`, `trip-plan.test.ts` and the relay's `hardening.test.js` + `verify-hardening.js`. One environment caveat applies symmetrically: neither relay test suite could be executed here (I5).

## 7. Recommended path forward

**Merge direction: pantryrun is the base.** The asymmetry is decisive. Porting into pantryrun means bringing over two client fixes, four test files and two config files — bounded, mechanical work whose correctness is verifiable by running the ported tests. Porting into grocery-app means reimplementing an entire security commit plus a Dockerfile plus a persistence volume, against a test suite that would not catch a mistake. Do the small thing.

Work is ordered by risk retired per unit of effort.

**Tier 0 — do these before anything else; hours, not days.**

1. **Fix the token malleability in both repos** (C1). Change `pool-server.js:280` and `:289` to key the spent set on `Buffer.from(parsed.nonce).toString('hex')`. Two lines, closes the only critical defect that is live in the repository you are keeping, and it must be done in grocery-app too if that tree survives at all.

   **Orchestrator-verified, with one deployment caveat.** The change was executed against pantryrun's real `UsedTokensStore`. Confirmed: it closes malleability going forward (a base64url re-encoding of an already-spent nonce returns `false`), and `hardening.test.js:261-272` **passes unchanged**, because it keys on an opaque string (`'permanent-token-xyz'`) and is format-agnostic.

   The caveat is a **one-time replay window across the entire existing spent set**. `checkAndMark` SHA-256-hashes whatever string it receives (`used-tokens-store.js:35,113`), so pre-fix entries are stored as `hashToken("v2.<base64>")` while post-fix lookups compute `hashToken(hex(nonce))`. Those never collide, and the legacy migration at `:75-82` does not bridge them — `isHashedKey` only converts raw keys to hashed ones, it has no notion of transport-string-versus-nonce. Verified by executing both paths against the real module: a token spent before the fix returns `true` (i.e. spendable again) on its first post-fix presentation.

   Close the window rather than accepting it: for one release, check both keys and mark the nonce key —
   ```js
   const nonceKey = Buffer.from(parsed.nonce).toString('hex');
   if (usedTokensStore.isUsed(tokenStr) || !usedTokensStore.checkAndMark(nonceKey)) { /* 403 */ }
   ```
   Drop the legacy `isUsed(tokenStr)` arm once every token outstanding at deploy time has expired out of circulation. Wiping `used-tokens.json` instead is strictly worse — it opens the same window for every token at once.
2. **Fix pantryrun's ABYTES line** (C5). `y-websocket.ts:433` → `const abytes = 16;`. One line, correct regardless of how the runtime premise resolves, and it stops silent loss of offline edits.
3. **Port grocery-app's `native-api-fidelity.test.ts` into pantryrun** (L9). This is the mechanism that found C5 and it guards the whole "mock more capable than the device library" class rather than one instance. It will also immediately flag `family.ts:424`'s use of `crypto_scalarmult_base`.
4. **Port grocery-app's `.gitleaks.toml` and `secrets:` CI job into pantryrun, with the `paths` keys stripped from both allowlist blocks** (H6 + H7). Stripping first is non-negotiable — copying it as-is imports the suppression. Change the trigger to `push: branches: ['**']` in both repos while you are there (M11).
5. **Replace pantryrun's `.gitignore:16-21` with grocery-app's block at `.gitignore:15-28`, and adopt its bare-filename bundle rules** (H8 + M15). Verify with `git check-ignore -v` on `.env.production`, `release.keystore`, `cert.p12`, `secrets.key` and a root-level `index.android.bundle`.

Tier 0 retires one critical and three high findings in roughly a day, and every step is independently verifiable.

**Tier 1 — before any deploy of the relay.**

6. **Bind the token rate limiter to the enrollment rather than the client-supplied deviceId** (H11), in both repos. This is the abuse-control anchor under C1 and C4; without it the token-issuance budget is whatever the client says it is.
7. **Add a container smoke test to both CI pipelines** — build, run, poll `/health`. Neither repo has one, which is the structural reason C2 shipped green (C6).
8. **Rate-limit `/enroll` by source IP** in both (L1), and authenticate `/health`'s aggregate counters alongside `/stats` (H4's tail).
9. **Close the OAuth pairing chain in both repos** (H5) or, faster and cheaper for now, document `ASSISTANT_INTEGRATION` as unsupported for production and leave it default-off. This is the largest single block of unfixed work either lineage carries and neither has started it.

**Tier 2 — only if grocery-app must survive as a deployable artifact.**

If grocery-app is being retired in favor of pantryrun, skip this tier entirely; that decision alone retires C2, C3, C4, H1, H2, H3, H4, H9, M4, M9, M10 and M12 at zero engineering cost, and is the reason the merge direction matters so much. If it must ship, port in this order, because the ordering has a safety constraint:

10. **`.dockerignore` first, then the Dockerfile COPY allowlist** (C2). Reversing this bakes `keys/issuer-private-key.pem` into a published image.
11. **The invite fixes as one change**: `familyFounderKeys` registry with its persistence hooks, the mandatory nonce, and the `${familyId}:${nonce}` replay Map (C3 + H2). H2's fix depends on C3's nonce requirement, so they cannot be sequenced apart.
12. **`used-tokens-store.js` wholesale**, including removing the load-time prune at `:53-59`, and flip `token-issuer.test.js:382-402` to assert the opposite (C4).
13. **`lib/collect-body.js` at all six sites**, prioritising `:555`, `:1440` and `pool-server.js:235` (H3).
14. **The capacity check** at `:633-638` (H1) and **`/stats`** at `:688-715` (H4) — both single-hunk changes.
15. **`hardening.test.js` and `verify-hardening.js`** (C6), so none of 10–14 can silently regress.

Schema downgrade detection before the two versions are ever installed on one device (M3); Android `network_security_config.xml` mirroring the iOS scoping already present (M5); the persist-failure surfacing gaps (M6); iOS build number automation (M7); settle the prebuild question empirically and stop guessing (M8); the invite-redirect scheme and package, plus the iOS placeholder in both (H9); drop `sentry-expo` (L8); delete `doc-store.js` and correct both compose comments and the README's zero-knowledge claim (L10, M13); port `dataWipe.ts` if grocery-app survives (M12); and correct pantryrun's stale sibling-repo sentence and mark its GC assertions as remote-verified-once (M2).

**One roadmap item, not a launch item.** Add state-vector reconciliation to the sync protocol (H10). Every durability finding in this report is permanent rather than recoverable because neither client can ask "what did I miss?" — closing that would defuse C5, C2's queue overflow, M3, M4 and TTL expiry in a single change. It is real design work and should be scheduled as such, but it should be scheduled, not discovered again in the next audit.
